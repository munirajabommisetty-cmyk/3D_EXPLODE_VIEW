# GestureLab CAD: 3-Minute Story Video

**Great Indian AI Internship Challenge 2026 by Gnani AI · Jugaad Genius Award entry**
*"The most creative or unexpected use of Gnani AI's models"*

**The idea in one line:** a student who can *see* a machine but can't *ask* it anything finally gets a machine that listens, shows and explains, in his own language.

**Storyline**

| Act | Time | What the viewer feels |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. The pain | 0:00–0:35 | "I've been that student." |
| 2. The turning point | 0:35–0:50 | "What if the machine could answer?" |
| 3. The machine answers (live) | 0:50–2:05 | "It actually works, and in Telugu too." |
| 4. Not just another chatbot | 2:05–2:25 | "This is different." |
| 5. Not just one student | 2:25–2:48 | "This could help so many people." |

*Ravi is an illustrative student used to tell the story, not a real user. Everything the app does in this video is real and shown live.*

## Act 1: The pain (0:00–0:35)

**On screen:** a dark screen, then a flat textbook-style diagram of a gearbox. Slowly fade into the real 3D planetary gearbox, still and silent.
**Caption (0:25):** `He can SEE the machine. He can't ASK it anything.`

> **Narration:**
> "It's eleven at night. Ravi, a diploma student from a small town in Andhra Pradesh, has his exam tomorrow. In front of him is a planetary gearbox. He can see every gear, but he has one simple question: *why is this gear here, and what is it connected to?*
>
> His textbook gives him a flat diagram. The videos he finds are long, generic, and not about the part he's looking at. A chatbot gives him a paragraph of text, but it can't see the gear he means. And Ravi thinks in Telugu, while every answer comes in English.
>
> He can see the machine. But he can't ask it anything."

## Act 2: The turning point (0:35–0:50)

**On screen:** the gearbox starts rotating; the GestureLab CAD workspace opens around it, with the voice tutor at the side.
**Caption:** `GestureLab CAD: what if the machine could answer?`

> **Narration:**
> "So we asked a different question: what if the machine itself could answer him? That's GestureLab CAD. Not a chatbot beside a picture, but a 3D machine that listens, shows and explains, in the language you speak. Let me show you what Ravi sees."

## Act 3: The machine answers (live, 0:50–2:05)

**Persistent caption:** `LIVE · real voice · nothing pre-recorded`

**Scene 1. He points (0:50–1:00).** Start the camera and point a finger at the Sun Gear, holding it. *(Safe alternative: click it in the parts tree.)* The Sun Gear lights up, and the inspector shows its role and what it drives.
**Caption:** `He points with his finger. The machine knows which part he means.`

> **Narration:** "Ravi doesn't need to know the part's name. He just points at it, with his own hand, through an ordinary webcam."

**Scene 2. He asks (1:00–1:30).** Press the mic once and speak naturally: *"Can you explain what this component is connected to and why that connection matters?"* Then stop talking. Recording stops by itself, and **"You asked · English"** shows the exact words. Then the tutor speaks: the connected gears glow in turn while **Gnani Timbre** explains each step.
**Captions:** `Gnani Prisma hears him and detects his language` → `Gnani Timbre explains while the parts light up`

> **Narration** (only before the tutor speaks): "He asks, the way he'd ask a teacher. When he stops talking, it knows he's finished. Gnani Prisma turns his voice into text and recognises he's speaking English. Now listen."
>
> *(Stay silent: at least 15 seconds of the real Timbre voice, with the gears lighting up.)*

**Scene 3. He switches to Telugu (1:30–1:45).** Press the mic and ask: *"ఈ భాగం ఏమిటి?"* **"You asked · Telugu"** appears in Telugu script, and the answer is spoken in Telugu.
**Caption:** `He switches to Telugu. No menu, no settings. It answers in Telugu.`

> **Narration:** "Then Ravi does what students really do: he switches to Telugu. There's no language menu. Prisma recognises Telugu on its own, and Timbre answers him in Telugu, keeping the technical words in English, just like his teacher would."

**Scene 4. His friend asks in Hindi (1:45–1:53, optional).** Ask: *"यह कौन सा भाग है?"* **"You asked · Hindi"**, and the answer is in Hindi.
**Caption:** `…and his friend Priya asks in Hindi.`

**Scene 5. He opens the machine and checks himself (1:53–2:05).** Spread two pinched hands; the assembly separates into an exploded view. Then show the tutor's two short quiz questions.
**Captions:** `Two hands: the machine opens up` → `Then the tutor checks if he really understood`

> **Narration:** "With two hands, he opens the machine to see inside. He can interrupt the tutor any time, like a real conversation. And at the end, the tutor asks him two short questions, to make sure he didn't just listen but actually understood."

## Act 4: Not just another chatbot (2:05–2:25)

**On screen:** split screen. On the left, a plain block of chatbot-style text (a simple graphic, not a real product). On the right, GestureLab CAD with the gears lit up and speaking.
**Captions, appearing one by one:**
`It sees what you point at` · `It shows while it speaks` · `It answers from the machine's real data` · `It checks you understood` · `It speaks your language`

> **Narration:**

Every answer is built from the machine's real parts data and checked before it's shown, and it's honest when something is reasoning rather than simulation. That's our jugaad with Gnani AI: Prisma and Timbre aren't just a microphone and a speaker here. They're the controls of a 3D lesson."

## Act 5: Not just one student (2:25–2:48)

**On screen:** the five models one after another: planetary gearbox, steam engine, reinforced-concrete building frame, truss bridge, human eye. Then a laptop with a webcam.
**Captions:** `Diploma · ITI · polytechnic · engineering students` → `Learners who think in Telugu, Hindi and more` → `Teachers and trainers` → `A laptop, a webcam, a browser. Nothing else.`

> **Narration:**

He's every learner who understands best in Telugu or Hindi. And he's every teacher who wants to show a machine to a whole class with just a laptop and a projector. It needs nothing more than a browser, a webcam and a microphone. A new subject is simply a new 3D model with its parts data. Today it's a gearbox, a steam engine, a building frame, a bridge and the human eye. Tomorrow, any machine, in any language Gnani speaks."

## Honesty rules (the challenge says: "no misleading demos")

- Ravi and Priya are **illustrative**. Don't present them as real users.
- Show **only what worked in your rehearsal**. If the Telugu or Hindi question fails, cut that scene; never fake it.
- The chatbot graphic in Act 4 must be a **plain, generic text block**, never another company's real product or logo.
- Never say the app is used by colleges, improved marks, or won anything. Say **"entry"**, not "winner".
- Never show a terminal, `.env` file, API key, notifications or personal data.
- Each live answer uses real credits (about 1 Prisma, 1 Gemini and 3–6 Timbre calls). Rehearse once, then record.

## Recording checklist

1. Backend and frontend running. Chrome maximised at `http://localhost:5173`, then **Ctrl+Shift+R**.
2. Microphone **unmuted** in Windows (Sound → Recording → Microphone Array → Levels). Good light on your hand for the gesture scenes.
3. Windows **Do not disturb** on. One browser tab, bookmarks bar hidden.
4. Record with **Win + Shift + R** (Snipping Tool), with **microphone and system audio** on. Record Act 3 as **one continuous take**.
5. Record the narration separately in a quiet room, at a calm, warm storytelling pace (about 150 words per minute).
6. Edit in **Clipchamp**: place the takes on the timeline above, add the captions, export **1080p, 16:9**.
